Independent research project. This website is not a U.S. government website, official agency product, or government-endorsed service. It uses publicly available information only. No access to internal government systems, restricted datasets, or authenticated government resources is required.

Purpose

The Federal CIO Intelligence Command Center brings together selected public cybersecurity and federal technology information in a navigable dashboard. Its purpose is to support awareness, further research, and source discovery, not to make operational or compliance decisions.

Who it is for

Anyone interested in publicly documented federal technology developments, including technology leaders, analysts, researchers, students, and cybersecurity practitioners. No account or sign-in is needed to view the dashboard.

Data sources and coverage

The dashboard may display selected records rather than complete coverage of all federal publications or cybersecurity advisories.

Collection and update process

  • Automated collection is configured to run daily using GitHub Actions; actual run times can vary.
  • The dashboard reads published data files from this project's public GitHub repository.
  • Collection timestamps and source-status indicators help readers assess freshness.
  • Historical trends begin when collection started. Earlier snapshots are not reconstructed unless explicitly stated.
  • Source failures or delays may leave previously collected information visible. Check the source's last successful retrieval time.

Change detection

  • Newly observed records are determined by comparing available collections.
  • “Newly observed” does not necessarily mean newly published, newly exploited, or newly effective.
  • Initial collections establish a baseline; comparisons require subsequent successful collections.
  • Automated labels and keyword matches are preliminary aids, not authoritative classifications or legal interpretations.

How to interpret the numbers

  • KEV catalog counts represent entries in the public catalog. They do not indicate that any particular organization is exposed, vulnerable, or unpatched.
  • Vendor comparisons show counts of catalog entries associated with vendor names. They are not vendor security ratings, risk rankings, or apples-to-apples measures of product quality.
  • Recent additions are based on catalog dates or observation windows as labeled, which may differ from original disclosure dates.
  • Federal Register matches reflect search results, not a verified list of binding requirements.
  • Executive briefs are automatically generated summaries and should be checked against the original linked sources before being relied upon.

Information boundaries

The project is designed for public information only. It does not solicit or require nonpublic government information, system inventories, credentials, internal security findings, procurement-sensitive records, or agency-specific exposure assessments.

Because the website and source repository are public, do not submit sensitive information through project contributions or issue reports.

Accuracy and limitations

Automated feeds can be delayed, incomplete, unavailable, or revised by their publishers. The dashboard does not independently verify all source claims and is not a substitute for official notices, security assessments, legal advice, or organizational decision processes.

For consequential decisions, consult the authoritative source and qualified personnel.

Transparency and project updates

The dashboard's source code, collection scripts, and published public-data files can be reviewed in the public project repository. Changes to the project are recorded in its GitHub commit history.

No personal biography, employer identification, or official government affiliation is asserted by this website.
